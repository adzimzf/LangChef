## ～ようになる
Expresses a change in state, ability, or habit over time. It means "come to be that ~" or "reach the point where ~." Often used to describe something you couldn't do before but can do now. The verb before ようになる is in the dictionary form (non-past plain form).
---
## ～ようにする
Indicates making a conscious effort or habit to do something. It means "try to ~," "make a point of ~," or "see to it that ~." It shows intention and effort. Negative form is ～ないようにする (try not to ~).
---
## ～ことになる
Expresses that a decision has been made by someone else, or a situation has been arranged or decided externally. It means "it has been decided that ~" or "it turns out that ~." It removes personal responsibility from the speaker.
---
## ～ことにする
Expresses a personal decision or resolution made by the speaker. It means "I have decided to ~" or "I will ~." This is a conscious choice. The past form ～ことにした is often used for decisions already made.
---
## ～つもりだった
Means "I intended to ~" or "I thought I would ~." It expresses that the speaker had a plan or intention but the result was different. It often carries a nuance of regret or an unexpected outcome.
---
## ～おかげで
Means "thanks to ~" or "owing to ~." It indicates a positive reason or cause that led to a favorable outcome. Can be used sarcastically for a negative outcome with a bitter tone.
---
## ～せいで
Means "due to ~" or "because of ~." It indicates a cause that led to a negative or undesirable result. It often places blame or expresses frustration about the cause.
---
## ～くせに
Means "even though ~" or "despite the fact that ~." It expresses strong criticism, contempt, or frustration. Used when someone acts in a way that contradicts their position, ability, or what is expected of them.
---
## ～わりに
Means "for ~" or "considering ~." It expresses that something is unexpected or disproportionate compared to a standard or expectation. It can be positive or negative surprise: "Considering X, Y is surprising."
---
## ～さえ
Means "even." It is an emphatic particle used to present an extreme example. "Even X is the case, so of course the rest is too." In negative sentences, it can mean "not even." The pattern ～さえ～ば means "if only ~, then ~."
---
## ～こそ
An emphatic particle meaning "precisely" or "for sure." It highlights a word and emphasizes that it is exactly that and nothing else. Often used in the phrase ～からこそ meaning "precisely because ~."
---
## ～なんて
A colloquial particle expressing belittlement, surprise, or emphasis. It can mean "things like ~," "such a thing as ~," or express that something is unexpected or not worth considering. It often follows nouns or plain-form clauses.
---
## ～だらけ
Means "full of ~" or "covered in ~." It carries a negative connotation and is used when something is covered in undesirable things (e.g., mistakes, mud, debts).
---
## ～べき
Means "should do ~" or "ought to do ~." It expresses a moral obligation or a socially expected action. It is a strong form of advice. The negative is ～べきではない (should not).
---
## ～わけだ
Means "that's why ~" or "so it means that ~." It expresses a logical conclusion based on the information provided. It is used to state something that naturally follows from a previous statement or situation.
---
## ～わけではない
Means "it doesn't mean that ~" or "it is not the case that ~." It is used to deny a possible conclusion or assumption. It softens a statement by saying "it's not that [extreme statement] is true."
---
## ～かねない
Means "might ~" or "could possibly ~." It expresses the possibility that something negative or undesirable might happen. It is a warning or a prediction of a bad outcome.
---
## ～ざるをえない
Means "have no choice but to ~" or "cannot help but ~." It expresses that the speaker is forced to do something against their will due to external circumstances. It's a formal, somewhat resigned expression.
---
## ～に違いない
Means "must be ~" or "I am sure that ~." It expresses a strong conviction or certainty based on evidence or intuition. It is a confident assumption.
---
## ～に決まっている
Means "it is obvious that ~" or "it goes without saying that ~." A very strong and colloquial expression of certainty. It asserts that the outcome is predetermined or absolutely certain.
---
## ～ものだ (1)
Expresses a general truth, a natural principle, or the way things inherently are. It translates to "should naturally ~" or "it is normal that ~." Often used to give advice based on common sense.
---
## ～ものだ (2)
Expresses a deep emotional recollection or a strong feeling about the past. Used with the past tense (～たものだ) to mean "I used to ~" or "those were the days when ~." It conveys nostalgia.
---
## ～ことか
Expresses a strong emotion, surprise, or deep feeling. It is often used with question words (e.g., 何度, どんなに) to emphasize the extent or degree of an experience. "How many times I...!" / "How much I...!"
---
## ～かと思うと / ～かと思ったら
Means "just as I thought ~, then ~" or "no sooner had ~ than ~." It describes two events happening almost simultaneously, often with an element of surprise or unpredictability.
---
## ～たとたん
Means "the moment ~," "as soon as ~." It indicates that right after an action is completed, another (often unexpected) action or change occurs immediately.
---
## ～うちに
Means "while ~" or "during the time when ~." It indicates that an action should be done while a certain state or period lasts, because the opportunity will be lost or the state will change afterwards.
---
## ～て以来
Means "ever since ~." It marks a point in the past after which a certain state or action has continued unchanged. It emphasizes the continuity from that past event up to the present.
---
## ～てからでないと / ～てからでなければ
Means "unless ~ is done first, ~ cannot be done." It expresses a necessary condition that must be fulfilled before something else can happen. "Not until after ~, can ~."
---
## ～として
Means "as ~" (in the role/capacity of). It indicates a person's position, status, or the function of something. "As a teacher," "as a representative."
---
## ～に関して
A formal expression meaning "regarding ~" or "concerning ~." It is used to specify a topic or subject matter. Often used in business or academic contexts.
---
## ～に対して
Has two main meanings: (1) "towards / against ~" indicating the direction of an action or feeling; (2) "whereas / in contrast to ~" showing a comparison between two things.
---
## ～に応じて
Means "in accordance with ~" or "depending on ~." It indicates that an action or result changes to match the preceding noun. "Depending on the weather," "according to ability."
---
## ～に従って
Means "in accordance with ~" or "as ~." It can indicate (1) following a rule or instruction, or (2) a proportional change where one thing changes as another changes.
---
## ～につれて
Means "as ~" or "in proportion to ~." It expresses a natural, gradual change where one thing changes along with another. It implies a correlation, not a cause-and-effect.
---
## ～に伴って
Means "accompanying ~" or "along with ~." It indicates that a change or event occurs together with another change or event. It is more formal than ～につれて and often used for large-scale or official changes.
---
## ～とともに
Has three main uses: (1) "together with ~" (same as と一緒に), (2) "as ~" (same as ～につれて), (3) "at the same time as ~."
---
## ～に基づいて
Means "based on ~" or "in accordance with ~." It indicates that an action, decision, or judgment is grounded in some fact, data, or standard.
---
## ～をもとに
Means "based on ~" or "using ~ as material/source." It indicates the source material or inspiration for creating something (e.g., a movie based on a true story).
---
## ～次第
Means "as soon as ~" or "depending on ~." When attached to a verb stem, it means an action will be taken immediately upon the completion of another action. When attached to a noun, it means "up to ~" or "dependent on ~."
---
## ～たびに
Means "every time ~." It expresses that whenever a certain action or event occurs, the same result or feeling inevitably follows.
---
## ～おきに
Means "every other ~" or "at intervals of ~." It indicates a regular interval of time or space. For example, 一日おきに means "every other day."
---
## ～ごとに
Means "every ~." Similar to ～おきに but often implies a less precise interval or is used for general repetition. "Each time," "every time."
---
## ～まま
Means "as it is" or "in the state of ~." It indicates that a state remains unchanged or that an action is done without changing the current state. "I left the window open."
---
## ～っぱなし
Means "leaving ~ on/doing ~ continuously." It has a negative nuance of neglect (leaving something on/out) or doing an action continuously without a break.
---
## ～かわりに
Means "instead of ~" or "in exchange for ~." It expresses substitution, where one action or thing is replaced by another. It can also mean "on behalf of ~."
---
## ～にかわって
Means "on behalf of ~" or "in place of ~." Similar to ～かわりに but more formal and strictly means a person acting as a substitute for another person.
---
## ～を通じて / ～を通して
Means "through ~" or "via ~." It indicates a medium, a means, or a period of time. ～を通じて can mean an entire period of time (e.g., through the year).
---
## ～にわたって
Means "over a period of ~" or "throughout a range of ~." It emphasizes a long duration of time or a wide range of space. Used in formal writing.
---
## ～にかけて
Means "over the period from ~ to ~." Similar to ～にわたって but implies a less continuous or more focused range of time or space. Often used in the phrase ～から～にかけて.
---
## ～に限って
Means "only in the case of ~" or "especially when ~." It highlights a specific case as an exception. Often used with negative outcomes: "(Why) of all days/people..."
---
## ～のみならず
A formal expression meaning "not only ~ but also ~." It is used in written language or formal speeches to add additional information.
---
## ～ばかりか
Means "not only ~ but also ~." Stronger than ～だけでなく, it emphasizes that not only one thing happened, but an even more extreme or unexpected thing followed.
---
## ～どころか
Means "far from ~," "let alone ~." It emphatically denies an expected action or state, stating that the reality is completely opposite or much more extreme.
---
## ～はもちろん
Means "not to mention ~," "~ is a given." It indicates that something is so obvious it doesn't need to be said, and that other things are also true. "A is obvious, but also B."
---
## ～上に
Means "on top of ~," "in addition to ~." It adds a similar piece of information (both positive or both negative) to intensify the situation. "It's expensive, and on top of that, it's bad quality."
---
## ～上で
Means "after doing ~" or "for the purpose of ~." When used with a past-tense verb (～た上で), it means the first action is a necessary prerequisite for the second. When used with a noun + の, it means "in terms of ~" or "regarding ~."
---
## ～最中に
Means "in the middle of ~" or "right in the midst of ~." It emphasizes that something (often an interruption or disturbance) happened while an action was at its peak.
---
## ～にあたって / ～に際して
A formal expression meaning "on the occasion of ~" or "when ~." It is used for important or once-in-a-lifetime events and is common in formal speeches and ceremonies.
---
## ～をきっかけに / ～を契機に
Means "with ~ as a start," "taking the opportunity of ~." It indicates that a particular event triggered a significant change or a new habit in someone's life.
---
## ～を問わず
Means "regardless of ~," "irrespective of ~." It expresses that something is true or applies equally to all cases, without being affected by the preceding noun (e.g., age, gender, weather).
---
## ～にもかかわらず
Means "despite ~," "in spite of ~." It expresses a strong contrast between a given circumstance and an unexpected result. It is similar to ～のに but more formal and emphatic.
---
## ～にかかわらず
Means "regardless of ~." Similar to ～を問わず but often used with verbs or adjectives in specific patterns. It means the outcome is the same no matter what the condition is.
---
## ～からいうと / ～から見ると / ～からすると
Means "from the standpoint of ~" or "judging from ~." It indicates the perspective or basis from which an opinion or evaluation is made.
---
## ～とすれば / ～としたら
Means "if it is the case that ~," "assuming ~." It is a conditional phrase used to present a hypothetical situation or a premise for discussion.
---
## ～ものなら
Means "if I/you could ~." It follows the potential form of a verb and expresses a strong desire to do something that is considered difficult or impossible. "If I could do ~, I would."
---
## ～ようものなら
Means "if one were to (mistakenly) do ~." It expresses a serious warning. If someone dares to do a certain action, a very bad consequence will certainly follow.
---
## ～まい
A negative volitional form meaning "will not ~" or "let's not ~." It expresses a strong negative intention or a guess that something probably won't happen. Used mostly in formal writing or set phrases.
---
## ～っけ
A sentence-ending particle used to confirm a vague memory or ask for confirmation about something you have forgotten. It is casual and often used when talking to yourself or close friends. "What was it again...?" / "...right?"
---
## ～ものか / ～もんか
Expresses a strong negative determination or denial. It means "I will never ~," "definitely not ~." It conveys a strong emotional refusal (often indignation or stubbornness).
---
## ～こと
A sentence-ending particle used in written rules or instructions to express a command or obligation. It is formal and impersonal. Often seen on signs or in manuals. "You must ~."
---
## ～ことだ
Means "should ~" or "it is advisable to ~." It is a way of giving advice or a recommendation. Softer and more personal than ～こと.
---
## ～ことはない
Means "there is no need to ~" or "you don't have to ~." It is used to reassure someone that a certain action is unnecessary or that a worry is unfounded.
---
## ～ことは～が
A pattern meaning "it is true that ~, but ~." It is used to partially acknowledge a statement before making a contrasting point or reservation. "I did go, but..."
---
## ～一方だ
Means "continues to ~," "more and more ~." It indicates that a certain trend or change is moving in only one direction (often a negative or uncontrollable one) without stopping.
---
## ～一方で
Means "on the one hand ~, on the other hand ~." It is used to contrast two different aspects or simultaneous events concerning the same subject.
---
## ～ば～ほど
Means "the more ~, the more ~." It expresses a proportional relationship where a change in one thing results in a corresponding change in another.
---
## ～くらいなら / ～ぐらいなら
Means "rather than ~," "I would rather ~ than ~." It expresses a strong preference for one (usually extreme) alternative over an undesirable one.
---
## ～よりほかない
Means "there is no choice but to ~." It is a strong, resigned statement indicating that the only available option is the one stated. Similar to ～しかない.
---
## ～ないではいられない / ～ずにはいられない
Means "cannot help but ~." It expresses an uncontrollable urge or emotional reaction. The action is done spontaneously, without conscious control.
---
## ～てしょうがない / ～てたまらない / ～てならない
Means "extremely ~," "can't help feeling ~." It expresses an intense, uncontrollable emotion or sensation. Often used with words of feeling (e.g., sad, happy, tired).
