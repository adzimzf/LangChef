## ～は～です
Usage: Used to state a fact or identity: "A is B." The particle は marks the topic of the sentence, and です is the polite copula (linking verb). Context: This is the most fundamental sentence pattern in Japanese. You use it for self-introductions (私は学生です - I am a student), describing someone's occupation or nationality, or identifying objects. The negative form is ～じゃありません (私は医者じゃありません - I am not a doctor). The question form is ～ですか (あなたは先生ですか - Are you a teacher?).
---
## ～の～
Usage: Particle の shows possession or that one noun modifies another. Context: It's equivalent to the English possessive 's or the preposition "of." Examples include 私の本 (my book), 日本語の先生 (a teacher of Japanese), and 東京の会社 (a company in Tokyo). This particle is incredibly common and is a basic building block for describing things.
---
## ～も～です
Usage: Particle も means "also" or "too." It replaces the particles は, が, or を in a sentence. Context: When you want to say that something is also true for another subject, use も. For example, if someone says 私は学生です (I am a student), you can reply 私も学生です (I am also a student).
---
## これ / それ / あれ
Usage: These are demonstrative pronouns for things. They stand alone and are not followed by a noun. Context: The choice depends on the speaker's and listener's relative distance to the object. これ is for something near the speaker. それ is for something near the listener. あれ is for something far from both. You use these to ask これは何ですか？ (What is this?).
---
## この / その / あの + Noun
Usage: These are pre-noun modifiers. They must be followed by a noun. Context: The distance rules are the same as above. この本 (this book), そのペン (that pen near you), あの人 (that person over there). You cannot say このは本です. It must be これは本です.
---
## ここ / そこ / あそこ
Usage: Demonstratives for places. Context: ここ (here, near speaker), そこ (there, near listener), あそこ (over there, far from both). You use these to ask for and give locations, a crucial skill for navigating a new place.
---
## Verb ます Form (4 Tenses)
Usage: The polite form of verbs, used in formal settings, with strangers, and in most classroom contexts. Context: You learn four patterns: present/future affirmative (食べます - I eat/will eat), present/future negative (食べません - I do not/will not eat), past affirmative (食べました - I ate), and past negative (食べませんでした - I did not eat). This form is the foundation for many other conjugations.
---
## ～から～まで
Usage: Indicates a range or span, for both time and location. Context: から marks the start point, and まで marks the end point. ９時から５時まで (from 9 to 5), 東京から大阪まで (from Tokyo to Osaka).
---
## ～へ行きます / 来ます / 帰ります
Usage: Movement verbs. へ (pronounced "e") marks the direction or destination. Context: 行きます (go), 来ます (come), 帰ります (return home). 学校へ行きます (I go to school). 日本へ来ました (I came to Japan). うちへ帰ります (I will go home).
---
## ～で行きます
Usage: Particle で indicates the means of transportation. Context: 電車で行きます (I go by train). バスで来ました (I came by bus). 歩いて行きます (I go on foot - note: 歩いて is the te-form of 歩く and does not use で).
---
## ～と行きます
Usage: Particle と indicates with whom you do something. Context: 友達と行きます (I go with a friend). 家族と来ました (I came with my family). This implies a mutual action. For a one-way action like helping someone, different particles are used.
---
## ～を～ます
Usage: Particle を (pronounced "o") marks the direct object of a transitive verb. Context: ご飯を食べます (I eat a meal). 本を読みます (I read a book). テレビを見ます (I watch TV).
---
## ～ませんか / ～ましょう
Usage: ～ませんか is a polite invitation ("Won't you ~?"). ～ましょう is a suggestion or offer to do something together ("Let's ~"). Context: 映画を見ませんか (Won't you watch a movie with me?). 一緒にご飯を食べましょう (Let's eat together). These are essential for making plans with friends or colleagues.
---
## ～で～ます
Usage: Particle で indicates the tool, method, or means used to perform an action. Context: はしで食べます (I eat with chopsticks). 日本語で話します (I speak in Japanese). It is also used for the location of an action (as opposed to に, which marks existence).
---
## ～に～をあげます / もらいます
Usage: あげます means "to give" from the speaker's perspective. もらいます means "to receive." The giver is marked by に. Context: 私は友達にプレゼントをあげました (I gave a present to my friend). 私は友達にプレゼントをもらいました (I received a present from my friend). The choice depends on who is doing the giving and who is the subject of the sentence.
---
## い-adjective / な-adjective
Usage: Two distinct types of adjectives with different conjugation rules. Context: い-adjectives end in い (高い - expensive, 大きい - big) and conjugate by changing the ending. な-adjectives require な when directly modifying a noun (静かな町 - a quiet town) and use です for the predicate. This distinction is crucial for correct grammar.
---
## ～が好きです / 嫌いです / 上手です / 下手です
Usage: Expressing likes, dislikes, and skill level. The object of the feeling or skill is marked by the particle が. Context: 私は猫が好きです (I like cats). 私は日本語が上手です (I am good at Japanese). The particle が is used instead of を because these are adjectives describing a state, not verbs describing an action.
---
## ～から (Reason)
Usage: Particle から connects two clauses to express a reason. Context: 寒いですから、窓を閉めます (Because it's cold, I will close the window). The reason clause with から always comes before the result. In casual speech, から can also be placed at the end of a sentence to soften the statement.
---
## あります / います
Usage: あります is used for the existence of inanimate things (objects, plants, ideas). います is used for animate beings (people, animals, insects). Context: 机の上に本があります (There is a book on the desk). 部屋に猫がいます (There is a cat in the room). This is a fundamental distinction in Japanese.
---
## Counter Words
Usage: Japanese uses specific suffixes to count different types of objects. Context: ～つ (general counter for objects), ～人 (counter for people), ～枚 (counter for flat objects like paper), ～本 (counter for long, cylindrical objects like pencils). Using the wrong counter is a common learner mistake, but using the general ～つ is often acceptable for beginners.
---
## Noun / Adj Past Tense
Usage: Past tense forms for noun and adjective sentences. Context: Noun + でした (past affirmative) / じゃありませんでした (past negative). い-adjective: 高かったです (was expensive) / 高くなかったです (was not expensive). な-adjective: 静かでした (was quiet) / 静かじゃありませんでした (was not quiet).
---
## ～は～より～です
Usage: Comparative structure: "A is more ~ than B." Context: 東京は大阪より大きいです (Tokyo is bigger than Osaka). The item being compared (A) is marked by は, and the reference point (B) is marked by より.
---
## ～で～がいちばん～です
Usage: Superlative structure: "A is the most ~ among/within ~." Context: 日本で富士山がいちばん高いです (Mt. Fuji is the highest in Japan). The category or group is marked by で, and the top item is marked by が.
---
## ～がほしいです
Usage: Expresses a desire for an object (a noun). Context: 新しい車がほしいです (I want a new car). The desired object is marked by が. This is a straightforward way to state a wish for something tangible.
---
## Verb ます形 + たいです
Usage: Expresses a desire to do an action. Context: 日本へ行きたいです (I want to go to Japan). 寿司を食べたいです (I want to eat sushi). Note that the object particle を can change to が when expressing a desire.
---
## Verb て形 (Basic)
Usage: The て-form is one of the most versatile conjugations. Its basic uses include making requests (～てください) and connecting sentences. Context: ちょっと待ってください (Please wait a moment). The conjugation rules vary depending on the verb group (godan, ichidan, irregular), and mastering them is essential for further grammar.
---
## ～てもいいですか
Usage: Asking for permission: "Is it okay if I ~?" Context: この本を借りてもいいですか (May I borrow this book?). 写真を撮ってもいいですか (Is it okay to take a photo?). This is a polite and common way to request permission.
---
## ～てはいけません
Usage: Expressing prohibition: "You must not ~." Context: ここで写真を撮ってはいけません (You must not take photos here). This is a strong and formal prohibition, often found in rules and signs.
---
## ～て、～
Usage: Connects two or more actions or states in sequence. Context: 朝ご飯を食べて、学校へ行きます (I eat breakfast and go to school). The tense of the entire sequence is determined by the final verb.
---
## Verb ない形
Usage: The negative plain form. It's used for negative requests and within more complex grammar patterns. Context: ここで写真を撮らないでください (Please don't take photos here). Like the て-form, conjugation rules vary by verb group.
---
## Dictionary Form + ことができます
Usage: Expresses ability or possibility: "can do ~." Context: 日本語を話すことができます (I can speak Japanese). ここで泳ぐことができます (You can swim here). This is a more formal and descriptive way to state ability compared to the potential form.
---
## た形 + ことがあります
Usage: Expresses experience: "have done ~ before." Context: 日本へ行ったことがあります (I have been to Japan). 寿司を食べたことがあります (I have eaten sushi). The た-form is the plain past form.
---
## Plain Form (Casual)
Usage: The informal speech style used with close friends, family, and children. Context: Instead of 食べますか (Will you eat?), you say 食べる？. This is a major shift in formality and is essential for understanding natural conversations in anime, dramas, and casual settings.
---
## ～と思います
Usage: Expresses the speaker's opinion or thought: "I think that ~." Context: 明日は雨が降ると思います (I think it will rain tomorrow). この映画は面白いと思います (I think this movie is interesting). The clause before と must be in the plain form.
---
## Noun Modification
Usage: Using a full clause to describe a noun, like a relative clause in English. Context: 私が昨日買った本 (the book that I bought yesterday). The modifying clause comes directly before the noun, and there is no relative pronoun (like "that" or "which"). This structure allows for very complex and descriptive noun phrases.
---
## ～とき、～
Usage: Temporal clause: "When ~, ..." Context: 日本へ行くとき、パスポートが必要です (When you go to Japan, a passport is necessary). 若いとき、よく旅行しました (When I was young, I traveled a lot). The tense of the verb before とき can change the nuance.
---
## ～てあげます / もらいます / くれます
Usage: These are auxiliary verbs that indicate the giving and receiving of favors (actions). They show who is doing something for whom, which is a key part of Japanese social etiquette. Context: ～てあげます means you do a favor for someone else. ～てもらいます means you receive a favor from someone. ～てくれます means someone does a favor for you (the speaker). The choice depends on the direction of the favor and the relationship between the speaker and the people involved. For example, 友達に宿題を手伝ってあげました (I helped my friend with homework), 友達に宿題を手伝ってもらいました (My friend helped me with homework).
---
## ～たら、～
Usage: A versatile conditional form that can mean "if" or "when." Context: 雨が降ったら、出かけません (If it rains, I won't go out). 家に帰ったら、すぐに電話します (When I get home, I will call you). It is formed by taking the plain past (た-form) and adding ら. It's often used for specific, one-time conditions.
---
